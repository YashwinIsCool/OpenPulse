## The 3-part story (use in any long-form piece)
1. **The shift:** AI now writes apps for everyone. Great — except the AI doesn't
   check whether the app is safe to ship, and most new builders can't either.
2. **The gap:** so millions of real apps go live with exposed keys, open data,
   broken logins, and "user A can see user B's data" bugs. The owners have no way
   to know and no one to call.
3. **The answer:** FixProof is the AI security engineer that finds those holes,
   proves them, fixes them, and confirms the fix — safely, with your approval.

## Objection handling (verbatim-ready)
- **"Isn't this just a scanner?"** Scanners give you a list and wish you luck. We
  prove each issue is real, then send you the fix to approve. The output is a
  solved problem, not a to-do list.
- **"Is it safe to let you test my app?"** We only test apps you own and
  explicitly authorize, inside a scope you agree to. Demonstrations are harmless —
  we never destroy data, never copy your users' data, never take your app down.
  Everything is logged.
- **"Will it change my code without me knowing?"** No. Fixes come as pull requests
  you review and merge. We never deploy anything ourselves.
- **"I'm too small to be a target."** Automated attacks don't check your size —
  they scan everything. Small apps with exposed keys or open databases are the
  easiest hits.
- **"I used a trusted AI tool, isn't it secure?"** AI tools optimize for *working*
  code, not *safe* code. "It runs" and "it's secure" are different tests.

## Words we use / words we avoid
- **Use:** proven, verified, fix, safe, authorized, plain-English, your approval,
  relief, closed.
- **Avoid:** hack (as a verb about customers), 100% secure, unbreakable,
  guaranteed, "military-grade," fear-bait stats we can't cite.

## Voice
Calm, competent, plain-spoken. Like a senior engineer who respects your time:
tells you what's wrong, what they did about it, and what you need to click. Never
condescending, never alarmist, never jargon for its own sake.
